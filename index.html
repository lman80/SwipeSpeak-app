Why a short walk makes you more creative

A short walk does more than stretch your legs. In a series of Stanford experiments, people came up with far more creative ideas while walking than while sitting down, and the boost lingered after they sat back down.

Researchers think the steady rhythm of walking frees the mind to wander. It doesn't matter much where you walk: a treadmill facing a blank wall worked almost as well as a stroll outside. So the next time you are stuck on a problem, the best move might be to get up.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no subscription. Cloud dictation and ✨ summaries use a free Groq API key (the Setup Guide gets you one in a minute). Offline dictation and reading aloud need no key at all.

Why does macOS say it can't be opened?

SwipeSpeak isn't distributed through the App Store, so macOS checks with you before the first launch. Open it once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only do this once; updates open normally.

What permissions does it need?

Accessibility so it can notice text you select and type your dictation, Microphone for dictation, and macOS's own Speak Selection switched on for reading aloud. The Setup Guide takes you to each one.

Which voice does it read with?

Your Mac's own Spoken Content voice. For the most natural sound pick a Siri voice in System Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content. The Setup Guide shows you where.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

Not yet. SwipeSpeak needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

How do updates work?

SwipeSpeak checks for new versions on its own and installs them in one click. Your settings and permissions carry over.
